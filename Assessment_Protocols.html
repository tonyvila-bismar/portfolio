Assessment Protocols

by March

VOLCANO MANOR — INTERNAL DOCUMENT

CLASSIFICATION: SERPENT-BONDED PERSONNEL ONLY

DISTRIBUTION: TANITH, PRAETOR'S COUNCIL, ACTIVE RECUSANTS (TIER 2+)

STANDARD PROCEDURES FOR THE PRELIMINARY ASSESSMENT OF CAPTURED TARNISHED OPERATING UNDER TWO FINGERS GUIDANCE

Compiled by Assessor Rethane, Volcano Manor Intelligence Division

Revised: Third Quarter Post-Bloom

INTAKE

The captured Tarnished is to be disarmed and escorted to an assessment chamber in the Manor's lower wing. Assessment chambers are numbered 1 through 6. Chambers 1 through 4 are equipped with standard restraint fixtures. Chambers 5 and 6 are equipped with serpent-bonded restraint fixtures, which are to be used only for Tarnished whose combat rating exceeds the threshold established in Praetor's Directive 11 (see Appendix C, "Combat Rating Assessment for Incoming Subjects," which was last updated seven months ago and should have been updated after the Altus incident but was not, because the person responsible for updating it was reassigned to perimeter duty and her replacement has not been briefed).

The subject's equipment is catalogued and stored in the adjacent holding area. All items bearing the mark of the Two Fingers are to be flagged for separate analysis. Items bearing the mark of grace — golden residue, luminescent patina, the specific warmth that characterizes objects that have been held near a site of grace for extended periods — are to be stored in isolation containers. The warmth persists. The containers do not eliminate it. They reduce it to a level that does not affect the assessing officer's concentration, which is the standard we have settled on because eliminating it entirely would require resources we do not have and have not had since the third quarter began.

INITIAL ASSESSMENT

The assessing officer introduces herself and states the Manor's position: the Two Fingers are a failed communication system. The Golden Order is a dead theocracy sustained by institutional momentum. The subject's service to either entity is a product of indoctrination, not choice. The Manor offers an alternative.

This introduction has been standardized since Praetor's Directive 3. The wording is prescribed. The assessing officer reads from the approved text. The approved text was written by Tanith and is effective. Tanith's prose has a specific quality of warmth that reads as sincere even when delivered by a third party reading from a card, which is a skill I have attempted to analyze and cannot replicate, and which I have noted in three separate quarterly reports as a single point of failure in our assessment pipeline. If Tanith is incapacitated, no current member of the intelligence division can deliver the introduction with equivalent effectiveness. This has not been addressed.

The subject's response to the introduction is recorded verbatim. Response categories are as follows.

Category A: Immediate hostility. The subject rejects the Manor's position and attempts to fight or escape. This occurs in approximately 40% of cases. The subject is restrained and the assessment continues. The hostility is not indicative of the subject's ultimate disposition. Hostility at intake correlates with long-term conversion at a rate of 22%, which is higher than the rate for Category B subjects (see below), a finding that surprised me when I first compiled the data and continues to surprise me, because it suggests that the Tarnished who fight hardest against the Manor's message are the ones most likely to eventually accept it, which is either a testament to the quality of our assessment process or evidence that the people most violently opposed to an idea are the people who have thought about it most carefully already.

Category B: Passive compliance. The subject listens, nods, agrees verbally, does not resist. This occurs in approximately 35% of cases. Long-term conversion rate: 14%. The compliance is tactical. The subject is waiting for an opportunity to escape or to gather intelligence. Passive compliance is the most difficult category to assess accurately, because the subject's behavior is designed to be indistinguishable from genuine openness, and the assessing officer must rely on secondary indicators — micro-expressions, speech cadence, the specific way a Tarnished holds their hands when they are accustomed to holding a weapon and the weapon is no longer there.

The hands are diagnostic. I have included this observation in training materials for new assessors. A Tarnished whose hands are relaxed is either genuinely disarmed or exceptionally well trained. A Tarnished whose hands maintain the grip pattern of their primary weapon — the curl of the index finger for a sword, the spread of the middle and ring fingers for a staff, the flat press for a shield — is holding a weapon that isn't there. They are still fighting. They will attempt escape within forty-eight hours in 78% of cases (n=43).

Category C: Engagement. The subject asks questions. This occurs in approximately 20% of cases. Long-term conversion rate: 61%.

The questions vary. Some are doctrinal — why does the Manor reject the Two Fingers, what is the theological basis for the Praetor's position, how does the serpent's blessing differ from the Erdtree's grace. These questions are addressed in the Standard Doctrinal Response Manual (Appendix D, which is current).

Some questions are practical. What does the Manor offer that the Roundtable Hold does not. What is the compensation structure for active Recusants. What are the conditions of residence.

Some questions are the ones I have stopped including in the quarterly summaries because they do not fit the response categories and because answering them honestly would require me to say things that the Standard Doctrinal Response Manual does not cover.

A Tarnished named Coreth asked me, during her initial assessment, whether the serpent was happy.

This is not a doctrinal question. The Standard Doctrinal Response Manual addresses the serpent's nature (divine, consumptive, liberating), the serpent's purpose (to devour the gods who have failed, to grow beyond the order that constrains), and the serpent's relationship to the Praetor (voluntary fusion, shared will, mutual apotheosis). It does not address the serpent's emotional state. The question presupposes that the serpent has an emotional state, which is not established in any Manor document and which, upon reflection, I realize I have never considered.

I told Coreth that the serpent is beyond such categories. She looked at me the way Category C subjects sometimes do when they have identified a gap in the assessor's knowledge that the assessor has not identified. She did not ask another question. She converted within the week. She is now an active Recusant operating in the Liurnian theater.

I think about her question more than the quarterly summaries reflect.

EXTENDED ASSESSMENT

Subjects who do not convert during the initial assessment are held for extended assessment, which lasts a minimum of fourteen days and a maximum determined by the assessing officer's judgment.

Extended assessment consists of three components.

Component 1: Doctrinal exposure. The subject is provided with Manor literature, including the Praetor's original writings on liberation through consumption, Tanith's commentaries, and selected testimonials from converted Recusants. The literature is effective. The Praetor's writings in particular have a quality that I can only describe as gravitational — they pull the reader toward a conclusion that feels inevitable even when the reader entered the room opposed to it. I do not know whether this quality is literary or sorcerous. I have not investigated. The distinction may not matter.

Component 2: Demonstrated alternative. The subject is permitted supervised access to the Manor's common areas. They observe the Recusants living, eating, training, socializing. They observe the quality of the food, which is good. They observe the quality of the company, which is varied. They observe that nobody in the Manor is required to pray.

This component has the highest correlation with conversion (r=0.71, p<0.01). The demonstration is more effective than the literature. The Tarnished who convert during extended assessment overwhelmingly cite the common areas rather than the Praetor's writings as the decisive factor. They say: I saw people who were not afraid.

I have included this finding in six consecutive quarterly reports. The intelligence division has not adjusted the assessment protocol to increase the duration of Component 2 or reduce the duration of Component 1. The literature remains the primary tool. The demonstration remains the secondary tool. The data does not support this prioritization. The protocol has not changed.

Component 3: Direct interview. The assessing officer conducts a series of one-on-one conversations with the subject over the assessment period. The conversations are semi-structured. The approved topics are listed in Appendix E.

Appendix E was last revised fourteen months ago. It does not include guidance for subjects who ask about the serpent's emotional state, or subjects who ask what happens to the parts of the gods that the serpent digests, or subjects who ask whether the Recusants who enter the serpent's maw are still themselves afterward or whether they become components of something that uses their memories without being them. These questions have increased in frequency over the past three quarters. I have documented this increase. The documentation has been received and filed.

DISPOSITION

Subjects who convert are assigned to the Recusant induction program. The program is effective. Graduation rate: 83%. Average time to first operational deployment: six weeks.

Subjects who do not convert are released at the Manor's outer boundary with their equipment returned and their memory of the Manor's interior layout suppressed through standard sorcerous intervention. The suppression is reliable. Recurrence of accurate recall: less than 4% within six months.

The release protocol was established by Tanith. The Praetor's original directive was not release. The Praetor's original directive was consumption. Tanith revised the directive on the grounds that consumed Tarnished cannot spread the Manor's message, while released Tarnished occasionally do, and that the conversion rate among Tarnished who were captured, assessed, released, and subsequently returned voluntarily is 89%, which is the highest conversion rate in any category.

Tanith was correct. The data supports her revision. The Praetor accepted the revision. The serpent, presumably, did not object, though the serpent's objections, if any, would not be recorded in any document I have access to, and I am not certain they would be recorded in any document at all, which is a gap in our institutional record-keeping that I have noted and that has not been addressed.

Whether the serpent is happy.

I am aware that this question falls outside the scope of institutional record-keeping. I am including it in a procedural document because I do not know where else to put it and because it has been asked, in varying forms, by eleven subjects over the past year, and because I do not have an answer, and because the absence of an answer is beginning to affect my capacity to conduct assessments with the confidence the role requires, which is a personnel issue and should be documented as such.
