The Company Town

by Kontextmaschine

lands-between-leftist: the roundtable hold is such a cool concept. a safe haven outside of time where warriors can rest and plan and find community. it's the one place in the game that feels like home.

It's a labor camp with a nice rug.

I'm not being flippant. I'm being descriptive. Let me walk you through the amenities at the Roundtable Hold — the one place in the Lands Between that the Two Fingers have set up for Tarnished use — and I want you to pay attention not to what's there but to what isn't.

What's there: a blacksmith. A spirit tuner. A finger reader. An incantation vendor. A guy who sells you information about other Tarnished you might want to kill. A woman who gives you hugs that are also a leveling mechanism. A door to the Two Fingers' chamber. And a basement where a man is trying to defile your corpse.

What isn't there: food. Beds. A market. A tavern. A library. A chapel for any purpose other than Two Fingers consultation. A healer. Entertainment of any kind. Windows.

The Roundtable Hold exists outside physical space. You can only access it through sites of grace, which are controlled by the Two Fingers' infrastructure. You cannot walk to the Roundtable Hold. You cannot stumble upon it. You arrive because grace brought you and you leave because grace takes you somewhere else. The Hold has no address. It has no geography. It exists in the space between the employer's permission and the employee's next assignment.

This is a company town.

I need to be specific about what a company town is because the term gets used loosely. A company town is not just a place where a company employs most of the residents. A company town is a settlement built by an employer, on the employer's land, stocked with the employer's goods, accessible only through the employer's infrastructure, where every service available serves the employer's purpose and no service exists that doesn't. Pullman, Illinois. The coal towns of Appalachia. The mining camps of the Witwatersrand. The common feature is not oppression — many company towns were materially comfortable — the common feature is

totality

. There is no part of the resident's life that is not mediated by the institution that put them there.

The Roundtable Hold is Pullman with a grace elevator.

Every service in the Hold exists to make you a more effective Elden Lord candidate. Hewg upgrades your weapons. Roderika tunes your spirit ashes. Enia reads the Fingers. D provides intelligence on undead threats. Corhyn and Goldmask research Golden Order fundamentalism, which is to say they're refining the ideology that justifies your employment. Even Fia — the deathbed companion, the woman who holds you — is a service. She gives you a consumable buff. The hug is a workplace benefit. It has a mechanical function. The warmth is the delivery mechanism for a status effect that makes you marginally better at killing the things the Two Fingers need killed.

There is no service in the Roundtable Hold that exists for

your

benefit as a person rather than your function as a candidate.

And the residents know this, in the way that company town residents always know it — not as explicit critique but as ambient background radiation that shapes behavior without being named. Diallos is there because he thinks he should be, rattling around with inherited weapons and no talent, performing candidacy the way a third-generation coal miner performs mining — because the town exists and the mine exists and what else would you do. Latenna needs transport to the Haligtree and uses the Hold as a waystation. Rogier is dying on a balcony and nobody moves him to a bed because there are no beds. The Hold provides mission-critical services. Comfort is not mission-critical.

Gideon Ofnir — the All-Knowing — is the company town's middle manager. His job is intelligence. He knows everything about every demigod, every Tarnished, every angle of approach to the Elden Throne. He shares this information not because he's generous but because informed candidates are more effective candidates and more effective candidates are more likely to produce the outcome the Two Fingers want. Gideon is HR. He onboards you. He briefs you. He tracks your progress. And when you actually get close to succeeding — when you threaten to actually

become

Elden Lord — he tries to kill you, because the company town's manager serves the company, not the workers, and the company has decided the position should remain vacant.

That last part is the thing that breaks the metaphor open. The Two Fingers don't want an Elden Lord. The Two Fingers want Elden Lord

candidates

— people in motion, following grace, fighting demigods, generating activity within the system the Two Fingers administer. An actual Elden Lord would restructure the system. An actual Elden Lord would close the Hold. The candidates are the product. The quest is the purpose. The company town exists to produce workers who never finish working.

Pullman had the same structure. George Pullman didn't build Pullman, Illinois because he wanted his workers to be happy. He built it because happy workers produced more rail cars. When the workers organized and demanded that the town serve their interests rather than the company's, Pullman cut wages and kept rents the same, because the town was never theirs. The infrastructure was never theirs. The rug was never theirs.

The Roundtable Hold's rug is very large and very round and sits in the center of the main hall and everyone walks on it and nobody owns it.

Hewg is chained to his anvil. I want to end on that because everyone slides past it. The blacksmith — the man who upgrades every weapon you will ever use, the man without whom no Tarnished candidate could function — is physically chained to his workplace. He doesn't remember who chained him. He doesn't remember why. He has been forging weapons for so long that the forging has replaced whatever identity existed before the chain. Ask him when a blade is done and he says "when it holds an edge," which is a quality standard, not an answer, and the difference is the entire condition of labor in the Roundtable Hold.

He can't leave. He doesn't want to leave. He doesn't remember that leaving is a thing that people do.

The company town works best when the residents forget there's an outside.
