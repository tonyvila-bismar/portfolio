The Cuckoo Problem

by Kontextmaschine

The Academy of Raya Lucaria has a private army and nobody thinks this is weird.

I want to linger on that. Not on the army — armies are easy, armies are just money pointed in a direction — but on the "nobody thinks this is weird" part, because it tells you something critical about how the Lands Between organizes violence. The Cuckoo soldiers are not the Academy's research assistants who happen to carry swords. They are not a campus security force that got out of hand. They are a fully operational military franchise attached to an educational institution, and the word "franchise" is doing exactly the work I need it to do here because a franchise is what you get when an institution wants the product of violence without the brand contamination of performing it directly.

The Cuckoos are named after a bird that lays its eggs in other birds' nests.

Think about that for a second. The Academy named its own military after a brood parasite. This is not a name chosen by people who are confused about what they're doing. This is a name chosen by people who find what they're doing intellectually elegant. The cuckoo doesn't build a nest. The cuckoo doesn't raise its young. The cuckoo outsources the entire reproductive process to a host organism and the host doesn't notice until its own offspring are dead. The Academy looked at this and said "yes, that's our military doctrine."

Raya Lucaria doesn't fight wars. Raya Lucaria outsources wars to a military force that operates under its seal but outside its direct oversight, garrisoned across Liurnia in positions that let them control trade routes, tax movement, and project force into communities that have no institutional relationship with the Academy whatsoever. The Cuckoos are not defending the Academy. The Cuckoos are defending the Academy's

interests

, which is a different thing, and the gap between those two concepts is where the Albinaurics live and bleed.

Here's how a military franchise works. You take an institution with legitimate authority — a university, a church, a crown — and you grant a subordinate organization the right to use violence on the institution's behalf. The subordinate gets the seal, the legal cover, the institutional prestige. The institution gets plausible distance from whatever the subordinate does with that authority. The deal works as long as neither side examines the arrangement too closely, because close examination reveals that the franchise's interests and the institution's interests diverged approximately forty-five minutes after the contract was signed.

The Cuckoos in Liurnia are garrisoned soldiers with no war to fight. Their nominal purpose — Academy defense — hasn't been tested since Radagon left and Rennala broke and the Academy sealed its own gates. The gates are sealed. The Academy is defended. The Cuckoos are still out there. Still garrisoned. Still armed. Still drawing on whatever logistical support the Academy's institutional infrastructure provides. And because they have no war to fight and no oversight mechanism that survived the Shattering and no incentive structure beyond "you have weapons and nobody is going to stop you," they do what every unsupervised armed force in history has done: they extract.

The Albinauric settlement in the Liurnia marshes is getting — and I'm quoting the only honest assessment I've ever read of the situation — "absolutely fucked on" by Cuckoo patrols. The Albinaurics are artificial people rejected by the Golden Order, living in a swamp because nobody else wanted the swamp, and the Cuckoos treat them as a resource extraction site. Not because the Cuckoos are uniquely cruel. Because the Cuckoos are unsupervised soldiers with an institutional mandate broad enough to cover anything they want it to cover and the Albinaurics have no institutional protection of any kind from anyone.

This is the East India Company problem. This is United Fruit in Guatemala. This is every military contractor who ever operated in a jurisdiction where the contracting authority couldn't or wouldn't enforce limits on the contract. The pattern is so consistent across human history that identifying it in a fantasy setting feels almost redundant, except that the fantasy setting does something the historical examples usually don't: it names the army after the parasite. The historical versions always choose heroic names. The fantasy version skips the euphemism.

What makes Raya Lucaria interesting is that the Academy doesn't need the Cuckoos. The sorcerers of Raya Lucaria are — individually, personally — more dangerous than any soldier. A senior glintstone sorcerer can do things to a human body that a platoon of Cuckoos couldn't replicate with a month of effort and detailed instructions. The Academy maintains a military franchise not because it lacks the capacity for violence but because it lacks the

appetite

for violence. Scholars don't want to patrol roads. Scholars don't want to shake down Albinauric settlements for whatever the Albinaurics have that's worth shaking. Scholars want to study. The Cuckoos exist because the Academy wants the economic and territorial benefits of military control without having to look up from its research to perform that control directly.

This is the cleanest example I've ever seen of the distinction between capacity and willingness. The Academy is capable of anything and willing to do nothing, so it subcontracts willingness to people who are willing to do anything and capable of just enough. The Cuckoos are the Academy's willingness, outsourced, and the outsourcing is the point — not a bug, not an oversight, not an unintended consequence. The Academy designed this. The Academy named it after a parasite. The Academy understands exactly what it built.

And then the Academy sealed its gates and stopped answering the phone.

The Cuckoos are still out there. The franchise holder has gone dark. The contract hasn't been revoked because there's nobody to revoke it. The soldiers are still drawing on institutional authority that the institution is no longer monitoring, enforcing, or even aware of. The seal still works. The name still frightens. The violence continues because the violence was never controlled by the institution — it was enabled by the institution, which is a different verb, and the difference between "controlled" and "enabled" is the entire history of colonial military administration in a single word.

Nobody recalled the Cuckoos. Nobody will.

The bird lays its eggs and flies away. That's the whole point of being a cuckoo.
