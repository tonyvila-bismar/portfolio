THE OMEN REGISTER

This article describes the standard identification, classification, and processing protocol for Omen-aspected neonates born within the jurisdictions of the Golden Order. For the theological basis of Omen classification, see Crucible Aspects (Reclassification). For the architectural specifications of subterranean internment facilities, see Shunning-Grounds (Leyndell). For the military application of Omen combatants, see Omen Bairn (Weaponization).

OVERVIEW

An Omen is a human infant born exhibiting one or more Crucible aspects: typically horns, less commonly tail growth, vestigial wing structures, or dermal scaling. Under the current theological framework of the Golden Order, these aspects are classified as defilement. Omen-aspected neonates are subject to mandatory processing under the Identification and Containment Protocol (ICP), maintained by the Office of Erdtree Communion and enforced by the Leyndell Census Bureau.

The ICP was established during the consolidation period following the War of the Giants, when the Golden Order standardized its definitions of sanctioned human form. Prior to this period, Crucible aspects were classified differently. The reclassification is outside the scope of this article.

Approximately 1 in 6,000 live births within the Altus Plateau jurisdictions produces an Omen-aspected neonate. The rate is higher in the Weeping Peninsula (1 in 4,200) and lower in Liurnia of the Lakes (1 in 9,100), where reporting compliance is inconsistent due to the Academy's administrative autonomy. These figures are drawn from the Leyndell Census Bureau's most recent complete survey. The survey is conducted every twelve years. Participation is mandatory.

IDENTIFICATION

Omen aspects are visible at birth. The attending midwife or chirurgeon is required to perform a Standardized Aspect Assessment (SAA) within the first hour of life. The SAA evaluates seven sites: cranial, spinal, caudal, scapular, costal, palmar, and plantar. Each site is scored on a three-point scale.

0: No aspect present. Normal sanctioned morphology.

1: Incipient aspect. Subcutaneous calcification, keratinous budding, or pigmentation anomaly consistent with early-stage aspect development. May resolve spontaneously within the first six months.

2: Manifest aspect. Visible horn growth, tail extension, wing cartilage, or scaling. Irreversible. Triggers mandatory reporting.

A composite score of 0 across all seven sites classifies the neonate as Sanctioned. A score of 1 at any site classifies the neonate as Watchlisted. A score of 2 at any site classifies the neonate as Omen.

The SAA form is filed in triplicate: one copy to the Leyndell Census Bureau, one copy to the Office of Erdtree Communion, one copy retained by the attending practitioner for a period of not less than twenty years. Failure to file within seven days of birth is a Category III offense under the Public Order Statutes.

Watchlisted neonates are re-evaluated at six months and twelve months. Approximately 40% of Watchlisted cases resolve to Sanctioned status. The remaining 60% progress to Omen classification. During the Watchlist period, the neonate remains in parental custody, though the parents are required to report any visible changes in aspect development within 48 hours. Compliance is monitored by local Erdtree Communion officers.

HORN REMOVAL

Upon Omen classification, the neonate is transferred to the nearest Erdtree Communion processing facility for horn removal. There are fourteen such facilities within the Altus Plateau jurisdictions and three in the outlying regions. The largest is the Communion Annex beneath the Avenue Balcony in Leyndell, which processes approximately 200 cases per year.

Horn removal is performed by a licensed chirurgeon-liturgist using consecrated instruments. The procedure involves excision of the horn at its base, cauterization of the growth plate, and application of a golden seal to prevent regrowth. The seal is renewed annually until the subject reaches skeletal maturity, typically between ages fourteen and sixteen.

The procedure is performed without anesthesia. The theological basis for this is detailed in the Liturgical Proceedings of the Second Erdtree Synod, which ruled that the pain of excision constitutes a purgative sacrament, cleansing the subject of residual Crucible influence. The ruling was unanimous.

Horn removal carries a mortality rate of approximately 8% for neonates under three months of age. The primary cause of death is hemorrhagic shock. The rate drops to approximately 3% for subjects between three and twelve months. For this reason, the ICP recommends scheduling the procedure between months four and six, though facility availability sometimes necessitates earlier intervention.

Subjects who survive horn removal are evaluated for subterranean internment or conditional surface release. The criteria for conditional release are detailed in the next section.

It should be noted that horn removal does not eliminate the Omen classification. A subject who has undergone successful horn removal remains Omen for all census, inheritance, and sacramental purposes. The procedure addresses the visible aspect. It does not address the classification.

CONDITIONAL SURFACE RELEASE

Conditional surface release is available to Omen-classified subjects who meet all of the following criteria:

1. Successful horn removal with no regrowth at twelve-month follow-up.

2. Composite SAA score of 2 at no more than one site at time of procedure.

3. No caudal or scapular aspects (tail growth or wing cartilage are disqualifying regardless of other scores).

4. Parental petition filed within 90 days of Omen classification, accompanied by a sworn oath of Erdtree fidelity and a surety bond equivalent to four years' household income.

5. Endorsement by the local Erdtree Communion officer.

Subjects granted conditional release are returned to parental custody under a monitoring regimen. Annual inspections are conducted until the subject reaches age sixteen. The inspection includes a physical examination, a re-administration of the SAA, and a doctrinal interview to assess the subject's integration into Golden Order worship.

The conditional release rate varies by jurisdiction. In Leyndell, approximately 15% of Omen-classified neonates are granted conditional release. In the Altus hinterlands, the rate is approximately 30%, primarily because the surety bond represents a smaller proportion of agricultural household income and because local Communion officers exercise broader discretion.

Subjects on conditional release who exhibit aspect recurrence at any point are reclassified as Omen-Recurrent and transferred to subterranean internment without further review. The surety bond is forfeit. Approximately 25% of conditionally released subjects are reclassified before reaching age sixteen.

The parents are not present for the transfer.

SUBTERRANEAN INTERNMENT

Omen-classified subjects who do not qualify for conditional release, and Omen-Recurrent subjects, are transferred to the Shunning-Grounds beneath Leyndell. Transfer is conducted by Erdtree Communion escort. The journey from outlying jurisdictions to the Leyndell Shunning-Grounds takes between four and twenty-one days depending on origin point. Subjects under the age of two are transported in standard containment cradles. Subjects over the age of two walk.

The Shunning-Grounds are a network of tunnels, cisterns, and repurposed sewer infrastructure beneath the capital. They were not purpose-built. The earliest sections date to the pre-Erdtree period and were originally drainage channels for the ancient city. Conversion to internment use began during the consolidation period and has continued on an ad hoc basis as the Omen population has grown.

Current capacity is estimated at 4,000 to 5,000 subjects. The most recent population count, conducted eleven years ago, recorded 3,812 living subjects. A more precise figure is difficult to establish because sections of the Shunning-Grounds are structurally unsound and access is restricted.

Subjects in the Shunning-Grounds are provided with baseline sustenance through a distribution system managed by the Erdtree Communion. Rations are delivered through chute mechanisms at fourteen distribution points. The nutritional standard is 1,800 calories per day for adult subjects and 1,200 calories per day for juvenile subjects. Compliance with the nutritional standard has not been independently verified.

There is no educational provision. There is no medical provision. There is no sacramental provision. Subjects in the Shunning-Grounds are classified as Abeyant under Erdtree Communion doctrine, meaning they exist outside the cycle of grace. They do not return to the Erdtree upon death. This classification was established by the Third Erdtree Synod and has not been revisited.

Mortality data for the Shunning-Grounds is incomplete. The most recent estimate, based on ration distribution volumes and projected population modeling, suggests an annual mortality rate of approximately 12% for juvenile subjects and 6% for adult subjects. The primary causes of death are structural collapse, inter-subject violence, and malnutrition. The Erdtree Communion does not maintain individual death records for Shunning-Grounds subjects.

Subjects born within the Shunning-Grounds to Omen parents are classified as Omen at birth regardless of aspect presentation. No SAA is administered. These subjects are not assigned census identifiers.

EXCEPTIONS: THE ROYAL OMEN

Two known Omen-aspected neonates were born to the royal house during the current age: Morgott and Mohg, twin sons of the divine lineage. Both exhibited severe Crucible aspects from birth.

Standard ICP processing was not applied. Horn removal was not performed. Both subjects were transferred directly to subterranean internment beneath the capital by order of the Erdtree Communion, bypassing the conditional release evaluation entirely.

The specifics of their internment are classified under Royal Household Seal and are not available through this registry. It is a matter of public record that Morgott subsequently assumed the title of King of Leyndell and served as the Erdtree's primary military defender during the Shattering. His Omen classification was never rescinded.

Mohg's current status is listed as Absconded.

RECLASSIFICATION HISTORY

The Crucible aspects now classified as defilement were, in the age prior to the Golden Order's establishment, considered marks of divine favor. Crucible Knights — the elite military order of that period — channeled these same aspects as incantations: horn thrusts, tail sweeps, wing manifestations. The transition from sacred classification to profane classification occurred during the consolidation period and was formalized by the First Erdtree Synod.

The synod's proceedings record no debate on the reclassification. The motion was introduced, seconded, and ratified in a single session. The session lasted forty-five minutes. The only recorded question, posed by a junior liturgist whose name is not preserved in the minutes, asked whether existing Crucible Knights would be subject to the new classification. The presiding officer ruled the question out of order.

The Crucible Knights were dissolved within the decade. Their incantations were reclassified as Aspects of the Crucible (Prohibited). Surviving knights were offered the choice between voluntary aspect suppression and honorable discharge to the hinterlands. The number who accepted either option is not recorded. The number who were subsequently processed under the ICP is also not recorded, because the ICP had not yet been established. A transitional protocol was used. Its records were not preserved.

AMENDMENTS AND CURRENT STATUS

The ICP has been amended fourteen times since its establishment. The most significant amendments are:

Amendment 3: Expanded the definition of Manifest Aspect to include subcutaneous horn growth detectable only by palpation.

Amendment 7: Removed the provision allowing Omen-classified subjects to petition for reclassification upon reaching the age of majority. The provision had never been successfully invoked.

Amendment 9: Established the Omen-Recurrent subcategory for conditionally released subjects who exhibit aspect return.

Amendment 11: Reduced the surety bond for conditional release from six years' household income to four years' household income, following a period in which conditional release petitions dropped to near zero in agricultural jurisdictions.

Amendment 14: Extended the Abeyant doctrinal classification to cover all descendants of Omen-classified subjects born within the Shunning-Grounds, regardless of aspect presentation. Previously, non-aspected children born to Omen parents in the Shunning-Grounds existed in a doctrinal gray area. Amendment 14 resolved the ambiguity.

The ICP remains in effect. No review is scheduled.
