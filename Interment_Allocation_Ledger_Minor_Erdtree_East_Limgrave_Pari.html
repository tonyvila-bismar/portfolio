Interment Allocation Ledger: Minor Erdtree, East Limgrave Parish

by March

INTERMENT ALLOCATION LEDGER: MINOR ERDTREE, EAST LIMGRAVE PARISH

Maintained by Parish Administrator Hilde.

Current volume: Year 409 through present.

Prior volumes: stored in the parish undercroft (Years 302 through 408, 7 volumes, continuous record).

PURPOSE

The Erdtree reclaims the dead. The roots draw them in. The faithful return to the cycle. This is the first principle of Golden Order eschatology and the organizing premise of this ledger.

Interment plots around the Minor Erdtree are allocated by faith-rank, which is assessed at the time of death by the parish deacon and recorded in this ledger. The faith-rank determines proximity to the roots. Proximity determines the speed and completeness of reclamation.

There are four tiers.

Tier 1: Inner ring. Within ten feet of the trunk. Reserved for ordained clergy, recognized saints, and individuals whose lifetime tithe exceeds 500 runes. Reclamation time: three to seven days. The body softens, the golden light enters the tissue, and the remains are absorbed into the root system. Nothing is left. The plot can be reused.

Tier 2: Second ring. Ten to thirty feet from the trunk. Available to confirmed faithful with a tithe history of at least 100 runes and documented attendance at sacramental services. Reclamation time: two to six weeks. The process is the same as Tier 1 but slower. A residue — primarily calcium deposit and hair — remains and must be cleared by the parish groundskeeper before the plot is reassigned.

Tier 3: Third ring. Thirty to sixty feet from the trunk. Available to baptized faithful regardless of tithe history. Reclamation time: two to eight months. The golden light reaches the third ring intermittently. Reclamation is incomplete in approximately 15 percent of cases, meaning that a portion of the body remains after the standard monitoring period. Incomplete reclamations are left in place for an additional year. If the remains have not been absorbed after one year, they are relocated to the fourth ring.

Tier 4: Outer ring. Sixty feet and beyond. No faith-rank requirement. This tier receives the unclaimed, the unchurched, the excommunicated, and the Tier 3 incomplete reclamations. Reclamation time: variable. The golden light reaches the outer ring rarely. In some years, it does not reach it at all.

ALLOCATION RECORDS: YEAR 412

The parish buried 41 individuals in Year 412.

Tier 1: 3 interments. All reclaimed within standard timeframe. Plots cleared and available.

Tier 2: 11 interments. 10 reclaimed within standard timeframe. 1 reclamation in progress (Month of the Boar interment, expected completion within two weeks based on observed golden-light penetration rates). Plots cleared as reclamations complete.

Tier 3: 14 interments. 9 reclaimed within standard timeframe. 3 reclamations in progress. 2 classified as incomplete after the standard monitoring period. Both have been relocated to Tier 4.

Tier 4: 13 interments (11 original assignments, 2 relocations from Tier 3).

Tier 4 requires a separate discussion.

TIER 4: STATUS REPORT

The outer ring currently contains 67 occupied plots. This number has been increasing. In Year 409, when this volume of the ledger began, the outer ring contained 38 occupied plots. The net increase of 29 reflects new interments minus reclamations.

Reclamation rates in the outer ring have declined. In Year 409, the golden light reached the outer ring on approximately 22 days. In Year 410, 14 days. In Year 411, 9 days. In Year 412, 4 days. The light is not coming as often. When it does come, it is fainter.

I have measured this by placing a strip of gold-reactive parchment — standard parish supply, used for detecting grace-presence in holy water — at six fixed points along the outer ring. The parchment darkens in proportion to golden-light intensity. The darkening has decreased by approximately 60 percent over the four years of this ledger volume.

In Year 409, 8 of the 38 outer-ring remains showed partial reclamation — some tissue absorption, residual golden coloration in the bones. In Year 412, of the 67 outer-ring remains, 4 show partial reclamation. The rest show no reclamation activity at all. They are simply buried dead.

This is not supposed to happen.

THE SECOND COLUMN

In Year 411, I added a second column to the Tier 4 entries. The ledger's standard format records: name, date of interment, faith-rank (or lack thereof), and reclamation status (active, partial, complete, or none). The second column records what I have observed in plots where reclamation is not occurring.

The observations are as follows.

Plot 29 (Alden, farmer, interred Year 406, no faith-rank): No reclamation. Remains intact. In Month of the Fox, Year 411, the groundskeeper reported that the grave soil had subsided, which is normal settlement. I inspected the site per standard practice. The soil had subsided, but the remains had shifted. Alden was interred supine, arms crossed. The arms were no longer crossed. The right arm was extended, palm down, as though bracing against the ground.

I recorded this as "settling disturbance" in the primary column and "positional change" in the second column.

Plot 34 (unnamed woman, interred Year 407, unclaimed): No reclamation. Remains intact. In Month of the Stag, Year 411, the gold-reactive parchment at station 4, nearest to Plot 34, showed a reading I had not previously observed. The parchment did not darken. It bleached. The golden-light reactive agent turned white, which according to the parish supply manual indicates exposure to "a grace-null field" — a zone where grace is not merely absent but actively excluded.

The supply manual does not explain what produces a grace-null field. The manual was written for detecting grace in holy water, not for measuring the theological properties of cemetery soil.

Plot 41 (Marden, carpenter, interred Year 408, baptized, Tier 3 relocation): No reclamation. In Month of the Hound, Year 412, I noticed that the grass over Plot 41 was greener than the surrounding turf. This is sometimes an indicator of accelerated decomposition — the remains enriching the soil. I tested the soil per standard method (auger sample, visual inspection). The sample was warm. Not sunlight-warm. Warm from below.

The second column for Plot 41 reads: "Soil temperature elevated. No identified heat source. Remains not inspected. I did not open the grave because I was alone and it was getting dark and the grave was warm."

I am aware that this is not a standard notation.

ADMINISTRATIVE NOTES

I have reported the declining reclamation rates to the district deacon. The district deacon has acknowledged my report. He has stated that the Minor Erdtree's grace-distribution patterns are a theological matter and that the Office of Golden Fundamentalism should be consulted. I have contacted the Office. The Office has acknowledged my inquiry and stated that grace-distribution monitoring is conducted centrally and that local anomalies should be reported via the standard form. I submitted the standard form in Year 410. I have not received a response.

I have not reported the second column.

The second column is not part of the ledger's standard format. It was not authorized by the district deacon. It records observations that the Office of Golden Fundamentalism has no form for, because the observations describe things that the Golden Order's theology says do not happen. The dead return to the Erdtree. That is the principle. The dead do not shift in their graves. The dead do not produce heat. The dead do not exclude grace.

The parish serves 1,400 people. They bury their families in the four tiers around the Minor Erdtree and they trust that the Erdtree will take their dead and return them to the cycle. I administer the system that makes this trust functional: the plots, the tiers, the faith-ranks, the parchment strips, the groundskeeper's schedule.

The system works for the inner rings. Tier 1 is flawless. Tier 2 is reliable. Tier 3 is declining but still functional.

Tier 4 is not working. And Tier 4 is where you put the people nobody else is willing to pay for.

I do not know what is happening in the outer ring. I do not have the training, the equipment, or the authority to determine what is happening. I have a second column in a ledger that the Office has not asked to see, and six strips of parish-supply parchment that are telling me things the supply manual was not written to explain.

I will continue to maintain both columns. The first column is the official record. The second column is what is actually happening.

The next entry in the second column is for Plot 29. Alden's right arm, which was extended in Year 411, is no longer extended. It is raised. The hand is approximately three inches above the soil line. The groundskeeper saw it first. He asked me what to do. I told him to leave it and to note the date.

The date was the 4th of the Month of the Boar, Year 412.

That was twenty-three days ago.

The hand has not moved since. It has not decomposed further. It is not reclaimed. It is just there, above the soil, reaching for something or away from something, and the gold-reactive parchment at station 3 is white and the grass around Plot 29 is very green and the grave is warm.
