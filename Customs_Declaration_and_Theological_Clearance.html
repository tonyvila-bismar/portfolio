Customs Declaration and Theological Clearance

by March

CUSTOMS DECLARATION AND THEOLOGICAL CLEARANCE

Vessel: The Sixth Remorse. Classification: Fog-Runner, Third Registry.

Port of Origin: Sellia, Eastern Territories (Caelid).

Port of Destination: Liurnia, Western Dock (unofficial).

Captain: Orvath. No family name on file. Previous crossings: 11.

Inspecting Officer: Deacon Harst, Office of Golden Transit.

MANIFEST — SECTION A: PERMITTED GOODS

Line 1. Dried meat, provenance unknown, 14 barrels. Captain states the meat is goat. The color is not consistent with goat. Theological status: animal products require no clearance provided the animal was not Crucible-aspected, rot-contaminated, or sacrificially consecrated. Captain has signed the standard attestation. Attestation accepted. Inspecting officer notes that Captain Orvath signs attestations the way a man deals cards.

Line 2. Trina's lily extract, 3 sealed casks. Soporific. Legal in Liurnia, restricted in Leyndell, unregulated in Caelid because Caelid does not currently maintain a regulatory apparatus for anything. Theological status: the Golden Order classifies Trina as an apocryphal figure. Apocryphal-associated substances are permitted for trade but may not be sold within five hundred yards of an Erdtree chapel. There are no Erdtree chapels at the western dock. Cleared.

Line 3. Smithing stones (grade 2-4), 40 units loose in burlap. Standard martial supply. No theological dimension. Cleared without inspection. The burlap is stained. The stain is not relevant to the declaration.

Line 4. Glintstone fragments, uncut, approximately 8 pounds total weight. Technically the intellectual property of the Academy of Raya Lucaria, which claims mineral rights over all glintstone deposits. In practice the Academy has not enforced mineral rights east of the Siofra border since the Shattering. Captain Orvath states the stones were surface-collected in Caelid. The Academy's position is that surface collection is still extraction. The Office of Golden Transit's position is that we do not adjudicate property disputes between a military theocracy and an academic monopoly. Cleared with notation.

MANIFEST — SECTION B: RESTRICTED GOODS

Line 5. Scarlet rot culture, sealed, 1 vial. Captain states the vial is a medical sample for a Liurnian alchemist studying rot-resistance. The vial is triple-sealed in wax, glass, and lead. It is approximately the size of a man's thumb. Theological status: the scarlet rot is classified as an Outer God contamination vector. Any material carrying active rot culture is restricted under the Contamination Protocols of Year 391. Transport requires a sealed-environment waiver, a named recipient, and a bond of 200 runes against accidental breach.

Captain Orvath has the waiver. It is signed by an alchemist named Drellis of Liurnia. The signature is neat. The waiver form is the correct edition. The bond has been posted in coin, counted and verified. Everything is in order. Inspecting officer clears the vial under restriction. Inspecting officer also notes for the record that this is the fourth rot sample Captain Orvath has transported in the past two years, each to a different named alchemist, each with immaculate paperwork.

Line 6. Root-resin, harvested, 6 blocks. Origin listed as "Deeproot Depths." Root-resin is organic material from the Erdtree or Greattree root system. The distinction matters. Erdtree-derived biologicals are sacred property, owned by the Erdtree Sanctuary, and cannot be commercially traded without a dispensation from the Fundamentalist office. Greattree-derived biologicals predate the Golden Order and are classified as natural resources, freely tradeable.

The root system in the Deeproot Depths is ambiguous. Some roots are Erdtree. Some are Greattree. Some are neither. The resin does not carry a label. Testing requires an arcanist. The western dock does not employ an arcanist. Policy in the absence of testing: classify as Greattree (natural resource) and clear for trade, unless the resin exhibits golden luminescence, in which case hold for testing.

The resin does not glow. It is dark amber. It smells like turned earth. Cleared.

Captain Orvath has asked whether, if the resin had glowed, holding for testing would mean confiscation. Inspecting officer has stated that holding for testing means holding for testing. Captain Orvath has nodded in a way that communicated understanding of a different sentence than the one that was spoken.

MANIFEST — SECTION C: PROHIBITED GOODS

Line 7. Deathroot, 1 bundle, wrapped in oilcloth, located in a hidden compartment beneath the aft cargo hold.

Captain Orvath did not declare this item.

Deathroot is classified as a Category 1 prohibited substance under the Fundamentalist Prohibition on Death-Adjacent Materials (Year 388). It is produced by the roots of the Erdtree in contact with the half-death of Godwyn. It drives animals and humans who consume it into frenzy. It is the physical manifestation of a theological contamination: the death that should not have happened seeping through the tree that was supposed to be eternal.

Category 1 prohibitions carry a mandatory penalty: confiscation of the substance, confiscation of the vessel, and imprisonment of the captain pending tribunal. There are no exceptions. There are no mitigating circumstances. The penalty was written by the same session of the Synod that classified Trina's lily as merely apocryphal, and it was written in the same week.

Captain Orvath, when informed of the discovery, stated that he did not know the deathroot was aboard. He stated that it must have been placed there by his previous cargo handler, who left his employment in Caelid. He stated that the cargo handler's name was Maren. He provided a description. The description matched no known persons in the transit office's records.

The deathroot bundle weighs approximately two pounds. It is fresh. Deathroot loses potency within ten days of harvest. The crossing from Caelid takes six. This deathroot was harvested no more than four days before departure, which means it was harvested after Captain Orvath's previous cargo handler allegedly departed.

Captain Orvath has been informed of this calculation. He has not amended his statement.

SECTION D: INSPECTING OFFICER'S NOTES

Captain Orvath has completed eleven crossings. His manifests are always complete for Sections A and B. His paperwork is always correct. His bonds are always posted. He has never been found carrying prohibited goods before this inspection.

The fog is two miles wide and takes between four hours and three days to cross, depending on conditions that no navigator has successfully modeled. Vessels enter the fog from one shore and exit at the other. Some do not exit. The Office of Golden Transit does not track the number of vessels that do not exit because the fog is outside the jurisdiction of any terrestrial authority.

Captain Orvath charges 40 runes per barrel for standard cargo. He charges 200 runes per sealed unit for restricted goods. His rates are known. His reliability is known. He is the only fog-runner operating between Caelid and Liurnia who has never lost a crossing.

The deathroot, at current market value in Liurnia — where it is consumed by a specific clientele that the Office does not officially acknowledge — is worth approximately 3,000 runes per pound.

The tribunal will determine whether Captain Orvath was aware of the deathroot. The tribunal's determination will be based on testimony, documentation, and the inspecting officer's report. This is the inspecting officer's report.

The inspecting officer notes, for completeness, that Captain Orvath's vessel is currently the only reliable transport link between Caelid and the western territories. Confiscation of the vessel would eliminate this link. The Office of Golden Transit does not weigh operational considerations in enforcement decisions. The inspecting officer mentions it because the report should contain all relevant facts, and this is a relevant fact.
