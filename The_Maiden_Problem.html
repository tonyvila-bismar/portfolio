The Maiden Problem

by Kontextmaschine

[reblogging myself from "The Company Town"]

Someone in the notes asked about Finger Maidens and I've been turning it over for a couple days because the more I look at it the worse it gets.

A Finger Maiden is assigned to each Tarnished. One maiden per candidate. The maiden's job is to convert runes into strength — she is, functionally, a payroll processor. The Tarnished kills things, accumulates runes, and the maiden converts those runes into advancement. Without a maiden, the runes are just currency with no bank. You can carry them. You can't deposit them.

The player character doesn't have a maiden. Your maiden died before you met her. Melina volunteers as a substitute, for her own reasons, and the game treats this as a minor early-quest inconvenience — oh no, your maiden is dead, here's a replacement, moving on. But the inconvenience is actually a revelation, because it tells you that maidens die. Routinely. In numbers sufficient that "your maiden is already dead" is a plausible starting condition for a Tarnished campaign.

Coreth — Rialle's maiden, mentioned in passing in one of the Oneila accounts — was 41 years old and had survived three previous Tarnished.

Three previous Tarnished. She survived them. They did not survive themselves. And Coreth moved on to the next assignment, because the institution requires maidens and maidens are assigned and when your Tarnished dies you get a new one the way a rental car company gets a new driver. Coreth is not a companion. Coreth is a renewable resource in a system that burns through the non-renewable resource (Tarnished) at a rate sufficient to require her services four times in a career.

Nobody talks about where the maidens come from.

This is the gap that should bother you. The Two Fingers maintain a supply chain of young women — and they are, as far as I can tell from every source, exclusively women — trained in rune conversion, theologically committed to Tarnished service, and deployed one-to-one with candidates whose life expectancy is measured in weeks. The logistics of this are staggering. How many Tarnished are called back from beyond the fog? Dozens? Hundreds? Each one needs a maiden. Each maiden needs training. Each training program needs instructors, facilities, curriculum, funding. There is an entire institutional apparatus dedicated to producing Finger Maidens and it is completely invisible because the game — like every institution that relies on feminized labor — treats the supply as natural. Maidens just exist. Maidens are just there. The question "who trains the maidens and where do they come from" is treated as no more interesting than "who stocks the vending machine."

The Roundtable Hold doesn't have a maiden quarters. The Roundtable Hold doesn't have a maiden at all — Melina appears and disappears at sites of grace, which means the company town doesn't even house its own payroll department. The maidens are gig workers. They're independent contractors in a system that controls every other variable.

The Two Fingers built an entire economy around Tarnished candidates and staffed the most critical support role with women who have no institutional home, no collective bargaining position, no visible career path beyond "survive your current Tarnished and get assigned another one," and the system treats this as so natural that nobody in the Lands Between has ever, to my knowledge, written a single word about where Finger Maidens come from.

Coreth knew. Coreth, at 41, on her fourth Tarnished, with three dead candidates behind her — Coreth knew exactly what the system looked like from the supply side. Nobody asked her.
