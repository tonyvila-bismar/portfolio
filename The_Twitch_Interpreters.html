The Twitch Interpreters

by Kontextmaschine

[reblogging myself from "The Institutional Mechanics Of Becoming Elden Lord"]

I got a bunch of responses to this one that were basically "okay but the Two Fingers are just a cosmic telephone, they relay the Greater Will's instructions and the system works because the instructions are divine" and I want to come back to that because it's doing something really interesting that I don't think the people saying it realize they're doing.

The Two Fingers don't talk.

I need you to sit with that for a second because we all just kind of slide past it. The Two Fingers are the Greater Will's authorized representatives in the Lands Between, the highest-ranking institutional actors in the entire Golden Order, and they communicate by twitching. Not speech. Not script. Not even the gestural systems that the Hornsent used for ritual coordination or the bell-language the fire monks developed for use in high wind. The Two Fingers are two enormous ancient fingers — already, already we're in a strange place — and they move. Slowly. At intervals that can stretch to decades.

And someone has to decide what the twitching means.

This is the Finger Readers. An entire professional class whose job — whose

entire institutional function

— is watching fingers twitch and then telling everyone else what God said. Not what God

actually

said, because God didn't say anything, God twitched, and the distinction between "said" and "twitched" is where the entire Golden Order lives and nobody talks about it.

There's a term in communication theory — I think Bateson used it, might have been Watzlawick — for the difference between digital and analog signaling. Digital signals are discrete: a word means a thing, a flag is up or down, a rune is present or absent. Analog signals are continuous: a gesture has magnitude, a tremor has speed, a pause has duration. Digital signals can be verified. "Did he say 'attack' or 'retreat'?" has an answer. Analog signals can only be interpreted. "What did the finger mean when it curled slightly leftward over the course of nine days?" does not have an answer. It has a reader.

The Finger Readers are the most powerful people in the Lands Between and nobody — including, I suspect, the Finger Readers themselves — understands this.

Because here's the thing about interpretation as institutional practice: it is indistinguishable from authorship. If I watch a large ancient finger curl three millimeters over the course of a month and then tell you "The Greater Will commands that the Tarnished be guided toward the Erdtree," I have not relayed a message. I have written one. The finger did not say "Tarnished." The finger did not say "Erdtree." The finger did not say "guided." The finger curled. Everything else is me. But the institutional frame — the robes, the title, the proximity to the sacred appendage — launders my authorship into divine reception. I'm not making policy. I'm Reading.

(The capital-R is doing a lot of work there and has been for centuries.)

You see the same structure everywhere institutional authority needs to be depersonalized. The Oracle at Delphi inhaled volcanic fumes and babbled; the priests of Apollo told you what she meant. The Roman augurs watched birds fly and told the Senate what Jupiter intended. Papal infallibility doesn't mean the Pope can't be wrong — it means that under certain formal conditions, whatever the Pope says

is

doctrine, because the institution has defined a mechanism by which a human voice becomes a divine voice, and the mechanism is: we say so.

The Finger Readers are augurs. They are haruspices. They are the Federal Reserve's communications department. They are the anonymous senior officials who brief reporters on background. They are anyone, anywhere, who stands between an illegible signal and a legible policy and says "what this means is —"

And they get old.

This is the part that kills me. By the time of the Shattering the Two Fingers are — and I'm using the clinical term here — fucked. The communication lag has stretched from days to weeks to months. The Finger Readers at the Roundtable Hold are interpreting twitches so slow they might be rigor mortis. And the Readers keep Reading because what else are they going to do? The institution exists to produce interpretations. If the Fingers stop twitching the institution doesn't dissolve — it just starts interpreting silence. "The Greater Will's silence on this matter should be understood as —" You've heard that one before. You've heard that one from every institution that has ever outlived its oracle.

Enia — the Finger Reader at the Roundtable — she's interesting because you can watch the mechanism in real time. The Tarnished shows up with a Great Rune and Enia tells them what the Two Fingers think about it. But the Two Fingers haven't thought about anything in what looks like months. Enia is performing a reading of a signal that may no longer be a signal, and the performance is flawless, because the performance is the point. The reading doesn't need to be accurate. It needs to be authoritative. The Tarnished needs to hear "the Two Fingers have deliberated and they want you to —" because the alternative is "two giant ancient fingers are sitting in a back room and nobody knows if they're still connected to the cosmic being they theoretically represent and the entire theological infrastructure of our civilization is propped up on the interpretive labor of an old woman in a shawl."

The Tarnished does not want to hear that.

Here's where it gets genuinely weird though: I don't think the Finger Readers are cynics. Cynics couldn't do the job. The job requires — structurally requires — that the Reader believes they are Reading, not writing. The moment a Finger Reader thinks "I am making this up," the interpretive authority collapses, because the authority rests on the Reader's own conviction that they are a conduit, not a source. This is why you can't reform the system from inside. The people inside the system are exactly the people who cannot perceive the system because perceiving the system would destroy their ability to operate it.

It's the same reason the best central bank communicators are the ones who genuinely believe monetary policy is a science rather than a rhetoric. The ones who know it's a rhetoric can't sell it.

There's a moment — I think about this a lot — where the Two Fingers at the Roundtable Hold finally move, finally produce some kind of signal after the Erdtree rejects the Tarnished, and the message is basically "wait." Wait. The Erdtree is sealed, the Ring is shattered, the demigods are eating each other, and the divine communication infrastructure of the entire civilization produces, after weeks of deliberation: wait. And the Finger Reader reports this with complete seriousness.

What's she going to say? "The Fingers twitched and I think it means they don't know either?"

The best part — the part that makes me want to frame this and hang it on my wall — is that the Three Fingers exist. A rival set of fingers, underground, associated with the Frenzied Flame, offering a completely different cosmic agenda. And the Three Fingers also don't talk. They also communicate through physical contact and ambiguous gesture. The theological alternative to the Two Fingers' interpretive regime is — another interpretive regime, with more fingers.

The Lands Between never had a direct line to the divine. It had interpreters. All the way down.

The Tarnished who becomes Elden Lord — any Elden Lord, in any ending — is the first person in the history of the Lands Between to interact with the underlying cosmic infrastructure directly, without an interpreter, without a Reader, without someone standing between the signal and the policy telling you what the signal means. And the first thing every Elden Lord does with that direct access is impose a new order — which will, inevitably, require new interpreters to explain the new order to the people who live under it.

Same as it ever was.
