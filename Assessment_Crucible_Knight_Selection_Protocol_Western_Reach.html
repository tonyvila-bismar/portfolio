Assessment: Crucible Knight Selection Protocol, Western Reach District

by March

Classification: Operational Review (Internal)

Distribution: Apex Optimate's Office Only

I.

The Western Reach maintains a standing complement of thirty-one Crucible Knights. Attrition from combat, aspect-related medical complications, and voluntary retirement averages 2.4 knights per annum across a twelve-year sampling period. Replacement requires selection, training, and fitting. The full pipeline from initial identification to field deployment averages six years.

This is not fast enough. A six-year pipeline against a 2.4 annual attrition produces a structural deficit that compounds over time. The Western Reach has operated below nominal strength for the last nine years.

The Temple does not acknowledge the deficit. The deficit is, in doctrinal terms, an expression of the Crucible's will: the precise number of knights at any given time is the number the Crucible intended. This position is logistically untenable and theologically convenient. I note it without comment.

II.

Selection begins at manifestation. When an individual produces a visible Crucible aspect — horns, scales, tail growth, wing buds, or less common expressions such as feathering, root-structure bone growth, or vestigial gill slits — the district temple dispatches an assessor. The assessor types the aspect, rates the density on a one-to-ten scale, and determines whether the individual meets the minimum threshold for knight candidacy.

The minimum threshold is six.

Individuals rated five and below are classified as low-density manifestants. They receive no institutional support. Their aspects are their own concern. Temple herblore services are available to them at standard rates, which in the Western Reach currently average fourteen silver marks per consultation. The median annual income in the Western Reach's agricultural districts is forty-one silver marks.

Individuals rated six and above are classified as knight-candidates. They are transferred to the district training compound. The transfer is compulsory. Refusal is technically possible under the Temple's own charter, but the charter was written in an era when refusal carried no practical consequences, because the alternatives to institutional protection for a high-density manifestant were substantially worse than institutional training. This remains true.

The transfer age ranges from eleven to sixteen, depending on manifestation timing. The median transfer age is thirteen.

III.

Training mortality in the Western Reach compound averages eight percent across the first three years. This figure has been stable for decades. The causes are, in order of frequency: aspect-related medical crisis during rapid manifestation (44% of training deaths), training accident (31%), infection secondary to aspect emergence wound (19%), and other (6%).

Eight percent is an acceptable rate.

I state this clearly because it requires stating clearly. The rate is not low. A system that kills eight in every hundred children placed in its care is a system that has decided what those children are worth. The Temple's position is that the training deaths are regrettable but inherent — the Crucible's gifts are not gentle, and the body's integration of new aspects is a physiological crisis that cannot be fully managed. This is true. It is also true that the compound's medical staff consists of three herblore adepts serving a population of between sixty and ninety candidates at any given time, and that a ratio of one adept to twenty-plus adolescents undergoing active bodily transformation is a resource allocation decision, not a theological inevitability.

The eight percent could be five percent. Possibly four. The cost would be two additional herblore adepts and a dedicated isolation ward for acute manifestation crises. The Temple has declined to fund this.

The unfunded positions are carried on the compound's operational ledger as "provisional allocations pending assessment." They have been pending for eleven years.

IV.

Candidates who survive the training pipeline are fitted for armor at year three. The fitting is a significant expense — aspect-rated plate costs approximately four times the standard infantry equivalent — and the Temple treats the fitting as a commitment threshold. A candidate who receives armor has been judged worth the investment. A candidate who does not receive armor by year four is reclassified.

Reclassification categories: (a) support corps — non-combat roles within the Temple's operational infrastructure, (b) medical discharge — returned to district of origin with a classification document that identifies them as a former knight-candidate, which in practice functions as a letter of introduction to the district's low-density manifestant population, or (c) administrative hold — candidates whose aspect development is ongoing but has not yet reached functional combat threshold, retained in the compound pending further evaluation.

Category (c) candidates may remain on administrative hold for up to three years. At the end of the hold period, they are reclassified to (a) or (b). The hold period was established to accommodate late-manifesting aspects, particularly wing-type, which can emerge as late as the early twenties in some lineages.

The compound currently holds fourteen category (c) candidates. The oldest has been on administrative hold for two years and eight months. Her aspect density was rated seven at intake. She has produced primary horn growth and moderate dorsal scaling but no caudal or secondary cranial development. Her combat rating, assessed quarterly, has been stable at 3.2 for the last four evaluations.

A stable 3.2 will not clear the threshold. The threshold is 4.0. She is sixteen years old and she will be reclassified within four months and she does not know this yet, because the notification protocol specifies that candidates are informed at the point of reclassification, not before.

The protocol is designed to maintain training motivation in candidates whose outcomes are already determined. This is efficient.

V.

The fitted candidates — approximately sixty percent of the intake cohort, after training mortality and reclassification — enter field integration at year six. Field integration consists of attachment to an active Crucible Knight unit for a minimum of twelve months, during which the candidate operates under supervision.

Field integration mortality is three percent. This is separate from, and additional to, the training mortality. The cumulative mortality from intake to independent deployment is approximately eleven percent.

Eleven percent of the children who enter the compound at thirteen do not leave it alive. An additional twenty-five to thirty percent leave it reclassified — functional, aspected, trained, but not Crucible Knights. They are the support corps, the medical discharges, the administrative holds who ran out of time. They received years of institutional training and will spend the rest of their lives in roles that do not require it.

The system produces, from every hundred candidates, roughly sixty knights and eleven corpses.

I have read these numbers several times. They are consistent across the sampling period. They do not improve. They do not worsen. They are the system's stable output.

VI.

The Temple's theological framework holds that Crucible aspects are gifts from the primordial wellspring of life. The selection process identifies those who have received the gift in sufficient measure. The training process refines the gift into martial capacity. The fitting process houses the gift in steel. The deployment process directs the gift toward the protection of the faithful.

At no point in this framework does the word "cost" appear.

The framework describes a gift economy. Gifts are received, refined, housed, directed. The recipients are grateful. The institution is generous. The deaths are the Crucible's will and the reclassifications are the Crucible's wisdom and the fourteen candidates on administrative hold are the Crucible's patience.

I understand this framework. I understand it because I operate within a framework that uses different words for the same arithmetic. The Arenam calls it honor. The Temple calls it blessing. The mathematics are identical: a system that requires a population of candidates larger than its output, because the difference between input and output is absorbed by mortality and reclassification, and the system's viability depends on the absorbed difference never being named as a cost.

I am not criticizing the system. I am describing it.

The distinction matters. A critic proposes alternatives. I am not proposing alternatives. The Western Reach needs thirty-one Crucible Knights. The pipeline that produces them kills eleven percent and reclassifies thirty percent. If the pipeline were gentler, it would be slower, and a slower pipeline against a 2.4 annual attrition rate would reduce the standing complement below operational viability, and a district without operational Crucible Knight coverage is a district that cannot defend its border, and a district that cannot defend its border is a district that ceases to exist.

The arithmetic does not accommodate mercy. I have checked.

VII.

One observation. It is not relevant to the operational assessment. I include it for completeness.

The compound's refectory serves meals at standardized intervals — dawn, midday, evening — to all candidates regardless of classification status. The category (c) candidates eat alongside the candidates who will be fitted. They train alongside them. They sleep in the same barracks. The only operational difference between a candidate on track and a candidate on administrative hold is a notation in the compound's personnel ledger, which the candidates do not have access to.

The girl with the stable 3.2 sits at a table with three candidates whose ratings are 4.4, 4.1, and 5.0. She does not know that her trajectory has diverged from theirs. She will learn in four months. She will be given a classification document and directions to the district's support corps intake office or, if she prefers, a medical discharge and a letter.

The refectory table will have an empty seat.

This concludes the assessment.
