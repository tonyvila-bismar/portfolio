The Inheritance Problem

by Kontextmaschine

The Shattering is usually discussed as a theological crisis or a family drama, and sure, it's both of those, but the thing nobody seems to want to talk about is that it's also a probate disaster.

Marika holds the Elden Ring. The Elden Ring is the operating system of reality. She breaks it. The shards — Great Runes — scatter to her children and stepchildren, none of whom were named in any will because there is no will because there is no probate law because why would you need probate law when the god who owns everything is functionally immortal?

This is the Lands Between's version of dying intestate, except the estate is the fundamental structure of causality and the heirs are demigods who can level cities.

And the response of the existing institutional framework — the Two Fingers, the Roundtable Hold, the entire apparatus of the Golden Order — is to send a bunch of resurrected exiles to go collect the assets by force. Which is not estate administration. That's liquidation. The Tarnished are not executors. They're repo men.

The thing is, every succession crisis in history follows the same pattern. The powerful figure dies (or in this case, shatters). The heirs grab what they can. The institutional framework, if it exists, tries to adjudicate. If the institutional framework doesn't exist — or if, as in the Lands Between, the institutional framework was designed to serve the now-shattered entity and has no contingency for the entity's failure — then adjudication defaults to violence. The heirs fight. Third parties intervene. The person who ends up with the estate is not necessarily the rightful heir but the one who was willing to kill the most people.

The Golden Order had centuries to develop a succession protocol and didn't, because the premise of the Golden Order was that Marika was eternal. Building a succession plan would have been an act of theological heresy — an admission that the god might fail. So they didn't plan. And now they're sending strangers to collect fragments of god from the god's own children, calling it a holy mission, and pretending there's a principled difference between "become Elden Lord" and "win the estate auction."

There isn't. The Erdtree is a courthouse that burned down with the deeds inside, and everyone's just grabbing what they can.

The demigods know this, by the way. Morgott guards the erdtree not because he has a legal claim but because he loves it. Rykard ate a snake. Mohg kidnapped his half-brother and built a dynasty in a sewer. Malenia is waiting at the Haligtree for someone who isn't coming back. None of them are litigating. They've all, in their own catastrophic ways, recognized that the estate is contested and settled on possession as nine-tenths of divine law.

Godrick is grafting arms onto himself, which is — if you squint — an attempt to increase his claim by literally incorporating other claimants into his body. That's not madness. That's mergers and acquisitions.
