Water Rights Adjudication: Wayford Village v. Chelmer's Reach

by March

WATER RIGHTS ADJUDICATION: IN THE MATTER OF WAYFORD VILLAGE V. CHELMER'S REACH

Magistrate's Court, Limgrave Eastern Circuit.

Presiding: Magistrate Orlan, Fifteenth Year of Appointment.

Decided: Month of the Hound, Year 413.

PARTIES

Claimant: Wayford Village, represented by Elder Githa. Population: approximately 340. Primary economy: arable farming (wheat, barley, root vegetables). Water source: the Siofra River, eastern fork, via the Wayford Mill diversion.

Respondent: Chelmer's Reach, represented by Headman Bryce. Population: approximately 210. Primary economy: mixed farming and livestock. Water source: the Siofra River, eastern fork, via a recently constructed upstream weir.

FACTS

The Siofra River's eastern fork has supplied Wayford Village's irrigation for at least two hundred years. The Wayford Mill diversion is the oldest engineered water structure in the eastern circuit. It predates the Golden Order.

Chelmer's Reach was established as a settlement in Year 396, when a group of displaced families from the Altus Plateau purchased land along the eastern fork approximately three miles upstream of Wayford. For the first twelve years, Chelmer's Reach drew water directly from the river by bucket and small hand-pump. The settlement's water needs were modest. Wayford did not object.

In Year 410, Chelmer's Reach constructed a weir across the eastern fork. The weir diverts approximately 40 percent of the river's flow into an irrigation channel serving Chelmer's fields. This diversion has reduced the volume of water reaching the Wayford Mill diversion by approximately one-third.

Wayford has filed a claim of riparian injury. The claim is straightforward: the upstream diversion has damaged Wayford's prior right to the river's flow, reducing crop yields in the two growing seasons since the weir's construction.

This much is standard water law. I have decided eleven riparian disputes in the eastern circuit. They are, as a category, tedious and predictable. This one is not.

THE COMPLICATION

The Siofra River originates on the surface in the Mistwood hills. It flows southeast for approximately twenty miles and then descends underground through a sinkhole near the Mistwood ruins. It reemerges approximately eight miles later, east of Wayford, and continues to the coast.

Prior to Year 407, the river that went underground and the river that came back up were, by all observable measures, the same river. The water was clear, cold, and chemically consistent with surface runoff from the Mistwood watershed.

In Year 407, something changed. The river that reemerges from underground is no longer the same.

It is still water. It is still clear. It is slightly warmer than expected — approximately four degrees above seasonal norms. It carries a faint luminescence visible only at night, a bluish-white shimmer that Wayford's residents describe as "the glow" and that Chelmer's Reach's residents describe as "unsettling." The luminescence fades within approximately two miles of the resurgence point. Wayford is three miles downstream. Chelmer's Reach is one mile downstream.

Chelmer's Reach's crops, irrigated with the river's water since the weir's construction, have grown at approximately twice the normal rate. Headman Bryce submitted yield records showing barley harvests in Year 411 at 190 percent of the district average and wheat at 210 percent. The soil at Chelmer's Reach is no better than average for the region. The seed stock is standard. The only variable is the water.

Wayford's crops, receiving reduced flow of the same water, have not experienced comparable growth. Elder Githa submitted yield records showing harvests at approximately 80 percent of historical averages — a decline consistent with the reduced water volume, but not showing the enhancement that Chelmer's Reach's fields display.

Chelmer's Reach's livestock will not drink the water. Headman Bryce confirmed this. The cattle, goats, and chickens are watered from a separate well. When offered river water, they refuse. This has been consistent since approximately Year 408. The livestock at Wayford, three miles downstream where the luminescence has faded, drink the river water without apparent issue.

THE RIVER UNDERGROUND

The court is not an arcanist's office. The court adjudicates property rights. What happens to the river during the eight miles it spends underground is, strictly speaking, outside the scope of this proceeding.

However. The parties have raised the matter, and the magistrate cannot pretend the river's properties are irrelevant to the dispute.

The Siofra River's underground course passes through what the Royal Geographical Office's records describe as "subterranean features of unknown provenance." A recent cartographic survey — the first ever conducted — reported structures, a population, and anomalous stellar phenomena underground. The survey was partial. The Geographical Office has not funded a follow-up.

The river, while underground, flows through or past these structures. What it absorbs, dissolves, or carries from that passage is unknown. The luminescence suggests contact with a substance or energy source not present on the surface. The warmth suggests a geothermal or arcane heat source. The growth-enhancing properties suggest something in the water that promotes biological activity.

No one has tested the water. Elder Githa requested that the court commission an alchemical analysis. The court does not have a budget for alchemical analysis. The nearest licensed alchemist is in Liurnia. Her fee for water analysis is 60 runes, plus travel. The combined assessed taxes of Wayford and Chelmer's Reach total 85 runes per year.

Headman Bryce has stated that he does not want the water tested. He did not explain why. When the court asked whether he was concerned about what testing might reveal, he stated that the water grows good crops and that he did not see the purpose of learning why good crops grow.

FINDING

The law is clear on one point and silent on the other.

The clear point: Wayford's prior riparian right is established. Chelmer's Reach's weir diverts water that Wayford has used for two centuries. Under the Limgrave Water Code (Year 288, revised Year 361), a downstream user with established prior use may not be deprived of customary flow volume by an upstream diversion. Chelmer's Reach must reduce its diversion to restore Wayford's customary flow. The court orders the weir modified to divert no more than 15 percent of the river's volume, which is the standard allowance for a settlement of Chelmer's Reach's size.

The silent point: what the water is.

The Limgrave Water Code was written for water. The substance flowing in the Siofra River's eastern fork is water in every respect the Code anticipates — it is liquid, it flows, it irrigates, it can be diverted and measured. The Code does not address water that glows. The Code does not address water that enhances crop growth at twice the natural rate. The Code does not address water that livestock refuse to drink.

If the water's properties are beneficial, then Chelmer's Reach's diversion has deprived Wayford not merely of volume but of an enhanced resource — and the remedy should account for that enhancement. If the water's properties are harmful in ways not yet apparent, then both villages are irrigating their crops with a substance of unknown composition, and the court should perhaps order a cessation of use pending analysis.

The court does not order a cessation of use. Both villages need the water to survive. Ordering them to stop irrigating during growing season would produce immediate food shortage to prevent a speculative future harm. The court has no evidence of harm. The court has evidence of crops growing very well and animals refusing to participate.

The court orders the weir modification as specified. The court further notes, for any future proceeding, that the Siofra River's eastern fork may not be the resource that the Water Code was written to govern, and that someone with more authority than a circuit magistrate should probably look into this.

The court does not have the mechanism to compel such an investigation. The court has a mechanism to file an advisory notice with the Office of Golden Fundamentalism, which is the closest thing to a scientific authority that the current administrative structure provides. The court has filed this notice. The court expects no response, because the Office of Golden Fundamentalism's current concerns do not include agricultural hydrology in a rural district of Limgrave, and in fairness, the court understands why.

The court closes this matter.
