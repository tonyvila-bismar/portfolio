The Fog Trade

by Kontextmaschine

tarnished-economist: why does nobody in this game talk about trade. there's merchants everywhere but no economy.

Because trade is boring and boring things are load-bearing. Always have been.

The Lands Between is an island continent surrounded by the Sea of Fog. The fog is not weather — it is a cosmological boundary, a liminal barrier between the Greater Will's jurisdiction and whatever's on the other side. Crossing it is dangerous, expensive, and poorly documented, which are the three words that describe every profitable trade route in history.

Orvath crossed it eleven times. Orvath was a pelt-buyer for the Great Caravan — a trade operation large enough to have its own papers, its own checkpoint clearances, its own bureaucratic footprint across every region of the Altus Plateau. His papers were so good that a Recusant assassin used them decades after his death and nobody checked. Nobody checks papers. They check seals.

The Great Caravan is one of those details the game drops and never explains. A caravan implies goods. Goods imply suppliers and buyers. Suppliers and buyers imply price discovery, credit, currency, contract enforcement — the entire invisible architecture that makes civilization possible and that nobody writes songs about. The Erdtree gets hymns. The trade route that feeds Leyndell gets nothing.

Here's what I can reconstruct. The merchants you encounter on every road in the Lands Between are a persecuted minority — their whole clan was buried alive in a catacomb and cursed. They trade anyway. They sit by the road with a blanket and some goods and they sell to anyone who stops, including the Tarnished who just killed forty soldiers a hundred yards away. This is not bravery. This is the merchant's calculation, the same calculation merchants have made in every war zone since Ur: the warlord needs supplies, and the person who supplies the warlord is the last person the warlord kills.

The interesting question isn't "why is there trade" — there is always trade, trade survives everything, trade survived the fall of Rome and the Black Death and it will survive the Shattering — the interesting question is what the trade tells you about what the Lands Between actually values. The merchants sell crafting materials, bolts, arrows, notes. They don't sell food. Nobody in the game sells food. Nobody eats. This is a theological economy — runes are the currency, grace is the infrastructure, and the things that trade are the things that serve the grace economy. The merchants are middlemen in a divine supply chain and their persecution makes perfect sense if you understand that an independent merchant class is a threat to any monopoly, including a theological one.

Orvath crossed the fog eleven times and nobody wrote down what he carried. Nobody wrote down what came back. The Sea of Fog is the Lands Between's greatest trade barrier and its greatest trade secret, and the secret is: we don't know what's on the other side because knowing would imply that the Greater Will's jurisdiction has an edge, and an edge implies an outside, and an outside is the one thing a totalizing theology cannot acknowledge.

The fog is a tariff that charges in existential dread. Pretty effective, as tariffs go.
