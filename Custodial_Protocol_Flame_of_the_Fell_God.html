Custodial Protocol: Flame of the Fell God

by March

CUSTODIAL PROTOCOL: FLAME OF THE FELL GOD

Mountaintops of the Giants, Fire Monastery Complex

Office of Sacred Containment, Erdtree Communion Authority

Current revision. Replaces all prior editions.

Distribution: Fire Monastery (all ranks), Office of Sacred Containment (restricted), Erdtree Sanctuary (summary only). Not for distribution to surface clergy, pilgrimage administrators, or secular authorities.

1. PURPOSE

This document establishes procedures for the ongoing containment, monitoring, and maintenance of the Flame of the Fell God, hereafter referred to as the Asset. The Asset is classified as a Category One theological hazard: an active divine principle capable of ending the current dispensation if applied to the Erdtree. It is the only such hazard confirmed to exist. There is no Category Two.

2. HISTORY OF ACQUISITION

The Asset was seized during the conclusion of the War Against the Giants, approximately nine hundred years before the current date. The war lasted eleven years. The Asset was not the war's cause. The war's cause was the Giants' refusal to acknowledge the authority of the Erdtree over their ancestral fire-worship, which the Golden Order classified as a competing theological claim incompatible with doctrinal unity.

The Asset could not be extinguished. Attempts were made during the first three years of occupation. All failed. The methods are catalogued in Appendix C (classified, available to Abbot-rank and above). The final assessment, issued by Queen Marika herself, concluded that the Flame was not a fire in any conventional sense but a divine intention expressed as combustion. You cannot extinguish an intention. You can only prevent it from finding its object.

The decision was made to contain the Asset in place, at the site of its original worship, under permanent guard.

3. THE CUSTODIAL ORDER

The Fire Monks were established in the third year of occupation. Their founding members were drawn from three sources: sixteen former Giant-priests who had surrendered during the final campaign and accepted conversion, twenty-two Erdtree clergy assigned from the Leyndell seminary, and an unrecorded number of local conscripts from the Mountaintop settlements.

The former Giant-priests were selected for their technical knowledge of the Flame's behavior. They understood its cycles, its responses to atmospheric conditions, its tendency to intensify during certain celestial alignments. This knowledge was essential to containment and could not be replicated by Erdtree-trained clergy, who had no tradition of fire-worship and whose liturgical training covered only the suppression of competing divine claims, not their management.

The former Giant-priests understood that they had been assigned to guard the fire they had once worshipped, against the possibility that someone might use it for the purpose their people had always said it served. They understood this because it was explained to them during the conversion process. The conversion process is documented separately.

The Erdtree clergy served as doctrinal supervisors. Their function was to ensure that the custodial practices did not gradually revert to worship. This concern was not theoretical. In the first sixty years of the monastery's operation, seven custodial monks were removed for conducting unauthorized fire-rites during night shifts. Three were former Giant-priests. Four were Erdtree clergy.

The distinction between tending a flame and worshipping it is liturgically clear and practically invisible.

4. CURRENT STAFFING

The Fire Monastery maintains a standing complement of forty to sixty monks, depending on seasonal conditions. Winter operations require additional personnel due to avalanche risk, supply-line disruption, and increased Flame volatility during the solstice period.

Recruitment has been exclusively from Erdtree seminary graduates for the past six hundred years. The last monk with Giant-lineage ancestry died approximately four hundred and thirty years ago. His name was not recorded in the personnel files, though his technical notes on Flame-cycle prediction remain in active use and are cited in the current monitoring procedures without attribution.

The monastery receives no pilgrims. It is not marked on any map distributed to the public. Its supply convoys are logged as deliveries to a "high-altitude weather station" in the Erdtree Communion's transportation records. The monks do not take a vow of silence, but the monastery's location, purpose, and the nature of the Asset are classified under the Seal of Sacred Containment, which carries a penalty of excommunication and Erdtree-denial for unauthorized disclosure.

Monks serve twenty-year rotations. Early discharge requires Abbot approval and is granted in cases of severe physical or psychological deterioration. The rate of early discharge has averaged eleven percent over the past two centuries. The rate of unauthorized departure — monks leaving without approval, typically by descending the mountain during supply-convoy windows — has averaged three percent. Monks who depart without authorization are not pursued. They are reclassified as "separated from service, Erdtree standing revoked" and their names are removed from the personnel register.

They are not listed as dead. They are listed as never having served.

5. MONITORING PROCEDURES

The Asset is monitored continuously. Three monks maintain direct observation at all times, working in eight-hour shifts. The observation post is located at a distance of forty feet from the Flame, behind a heat-shielding wall constructed of Erdtree-blessed stone that requires replacement every nine to twelve years due to thermal degradation.

Monitoring consists of visual assessment (color, height, behavior), thermal measurement (using graduated iron rods inserted into the Flame's periphery at standardized intervals), and atmospheric sampling (air drawn from the Flame's vicinity is passed through a series of filters that measure particulate content, which correlates with Flame intensity on a scale developed by the original Giant-priest custodians and never independently verified).

Monks assigned to observation shifts report a cumulative psychological effect described in internal literature as "proximity fatigue." Symptoms include insomnia, involuntary vocalization during sleep, a persistent sensation of being addressed, and, in approximately fifteen percent of long-serving observers, the development of a conviction that the Flame is aware of being watched. This conviction is classified as an occupational hazard rather than a doctrinal concern. Monks experiencing it are rotated to non-observation duties for a minimum of ninety days.

The conviction does not typically resolve after rotation. Monks who have experienced it tend to volunteer for observation shifts at a higher rate than those who have not.

6. CONTAINMENT INTEGRITY

The Asset has not breached containment in the nine hundred years of custodial operation. This is the monastery's primary achievement and the justification for its continued existence.

The Asset has, however, fluctuated. Seventeen major fluctuation events are recorded in the monastery's logs. A major fluctuation is defined as a sustained increase in Flame height exceeding twice the baseline for a period of more than six hours. The most recent major fluctuation occurred approximately one hundred and twenty years ago, during a period that coincided with, though is not confirmed to have been caused by, the death of an Erdtree demigod in a distant region.

During fluctuation events, the Flame produces audible output. The sound has been described variously as "roaring," "singing," "a voice speaking a language that was understood but not remembered afterward," and "the sound a furnace makes when the door is opened, if the furnace were the size of a cathedral." There is no consensus on whether the sound constitutes communication. The monastery's position, maintained since the founding, is that the sound is a thermoacoustic phenomenon and does not warrant theological interpretation.

One monk, during the fluctuation of Year 614, transcribed the sound phonetically. His transcription runs to eleven pages. When compared against surviving fragments of the Giant liturgical language — fragments preserved, with some irony, in the monastery's own technical manuals — the phonetic transcription corresponds to a prayer traditionally recited at the annual fire-festival.

The transcription is filed in the restricted archive. It has not been destroyed because destroying documents under the Seal of Sacred Containment requires authorization from the Erdtree Sanctuary in Leyndell, and requesting such authorization would require disclosing the document's contents, which would require disclosing that the Asset produces output corresponding to Giant liturgical tradition, which would raise questions about the monastery's classification of the sound as a thermoacoustic phenomenon.

The document remains filed. It has not been read since it was transcribed.

7. CONTINGENCY PROTOCOLS

In the event of a full containment breach — defined as the Asset leaving the monastery complex under its own volition or through external intervention — the monastery's protocols are as follows:

The observation team is to document the breach in real time, recording direction, velocity, and behavior of the Asset.

The Abbot is to transmit a priority dispatch to the Erdtree Sanctuary via the mountain relay system.

All personnel are to evacuate to the secondary compound, located three miles east.

No monk is authorized to attempt to stop, redirect, or interact with the Asset during a breach event.

These protocols have never been tested. The monastery conducts annual breach drills, but the drills do not simulate the Asset's actual behavior because the Asset's behavior during a breach cannot be predicted. The drills simulate an orderly evacuation from a stationary fire. The Asset is not a stationary fire.

The relay system to Leyndell has not been tested in seventy years. The most recent test, which involved sending a routine status message, received no acknowledgment. A follow-up inquiry established that the relay's receiving station in Leyndell had been converted to a grain storage facility at an unrecorded date. The monastery filed a report requesting restoration of the relay. The report was acknowledged. The relay has not been restored.

8. THEOLOGICAL STATUS

The Asset is classified as "Sealed Divine Residuum, Hostile." This classification was established at the Second Erdtree Synod and has not been revisited.

The classification presumes that the Flame is a remnant of a defeated divine order — the theological equivalent of unexploded ordnance. The monastery's function is analogous to a bomb-disposal unit assigned to a device that cannot be defused: maintain distance, monitor for changes, prevent civilian access, and wait.

The classification does not address the possibility that the Asset is not residuum but a living divine principle — that the Fell God's fire was not defeated but contained, and that containment is not the same as victory. This possibility is not addressed because addressing it would require acknowledging that the War Against the Giants did not achieve its stated objective, which was the elimination of the competing theological claim.

The Flame is still burning. The claim is still being made. The monks who guard it can hear it, particularly during fluctuation events, and particularly during the solstice, and particularly at night.

9. CURRENT STATUS

Operational. Fully staffed. No fluctuation events in the current reporting period.

The Abbot notes in his quarterly assessment that morale is adequate, supply lines are functioning, and the Flame continues to burn at baseline intensity. He notes that three monks have requested early discharge in the current cycle, which is within normal parameters. He notes that observation-shift volunteers continue to exceed requirements.

He does not note that the current cohort includes no monks who have studied the Giant liturgical language, because the Giant liturgical language is not part of the Erdtree seminary curriculum, because it is a dead language belonging to a destroyed civilization whose divine claim has been classified as eliminated.

The technical manuals used to monitor the Flame are written in it.
